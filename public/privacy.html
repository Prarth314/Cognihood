1. Scope and roles

This notice applies to the CogniHood web application, its hosted website, account system, optional cloud-enhanced analysis, and related support interactions. It does not govern third-party websites linked from CogniHood or a separately operated installation of the MIT-licensed source code.

For the hosted development service, the CogniHood operator determines why and how application data is processed. Vercel, Supabase, Google, and their infrastructure providers process certain data to provide hosting, authentication, database, model-delivery, and optional AI services.

3. Camera, face geometry, and optional cloud analysis

Camera processing may be sensitive. Some jurisdictions may regulate face geometry, face landmarks, or inferences from camera data as biometric or sensitive information even when the system does not identify a person.

  • Local by default: live frames and MediaPipe face landmarks are processed in browser memory. CogniHood does not intentionally write raw video to its application database.
  • No identity recognition: the current application is not designed to establish identity, compare faces, create a reusable face-recognition template, or identify a person across sessions.
  • Permission: the browser requests camera permission. You may deny or revoke it, but camera-dependent features will not work.
  • Optional cloud enhancement: only after a separate per-session choice, compressed still frames may be sent to an authenticated Supabase Edge Function and then to the configured Google Gemini API.
  • Vendor handling: Google’s processing and retention depend on the API plan and applicable Google terms. Unpaid Gemini services may permit product-improvement use and human review; production operation must use an appropriate commercial configuration and must not submit data until that configuration is verified.
  • Stop control: ending a session or disabling cloud analysis stops new optional cloud-frame submissions. Browser and vendor logs may persist according to their own retention rules.

4. Why information is used

  • Authenticate accounts and prevent unauthorized access.
  • Provide local camera-signal checks, experimental attention indicators, warnings, trip summaries, archives, and personal historical references.
  • Provide optional cloud-enhanced interpretation when separately enabled.
  • Preserve user settings, migrate older local records, export data, and delete accounts.
  • Secure, debug, rate-limit, maintain, and improve the service.
  • Measure aggregate website usage with Vercel Web Analytics.
  • Comply with law, enforce terms, investigate abuse, and protect users and the service.

CogniHood does not use its indicators to make legal, employment, insurance, credit, healthcare, or similarly significant decisions about a person. It does not sell personal information or use it for cross-context behavioral advertising.

5. Legal bases where required

Depending on jurisdiction and feature, processing may rely on performance of the requested service, consent (including camera permission and optional cloud analysis), legitimate interests in security and service operation, and compliance with legal obligations. Consent may be withdrawn for future optional processing. Withdrawal does not affect processing already lawfully completed.

6. When information is disclosed

Information may be disclosed to the service providers listed in the Service Providers Notice, to a successor in a properly governed business transaction, when you direct or consent to disclosure, or when reasonably necessary to comply with law, protect rights and safety, or investigate abuse. CogniHood does not currently sell personal information, share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, or rent user lists.

8. Your choices and privacy rights

Depending on applicable law, you may have rights to access, know, correct, export, delete, restrict, object, withdraw consent, opt out of sale or targeted advertising, appeal a refusal, and lodge a complaint with a regulator. CogniHood will not discriminate against a user for exercising an applicable privacy right.

  • Use the in-product export feature to obtain available account records.
  • Use the in-product account-deletion control to request permanent deletion of the account and associated CogniHood tables.
  • Disable cloud analysis for a session or revoke camera access through browser settings.
  • Clear local storage and IndexedDB through browser controls.

For a request not supported in-product, contact the maintainer through the method listed on the GitHub profile and request a private channel. Do not post sensitive data publicly. Identity verification may be required. An authorized agent may need to provide proof of authority.

9. Regional disclosures

California

The table in Section 2 describes categories of identifiers, internet activity, audiovisual information, inferences, and potentially sensitive information processed during the preceding 12 months. CogniHood does not knowingly sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising. Where applicable, California residents may request access, correction, deletion, portability, limitation of sensitive-information use, and non-discrimination. Texas and other U.S. states

State laws may provide rights to confirm processing, access, correct, delete, obtain portable data, opt out of certain targeted advertising, sale, or profiling, and appeal a decision. CogniHood does not use face data for unique identification, but camera-derived face geometry may still trigger special notice, consent, security, and destruction obligations. Commercial deployment requires state-by-state counsel review.

EEA, United Kingdom, and Switzerland

Users may have rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, consent withdrawal, and complaint to a supervisory authority. CogniHood does not make decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects solely through automated processing. An EU/UK representative, transfer assessment, vendor data-processing agreements, and confirmed lawful bases are not yet in place; the current development service should not be marketed in those regions until these items are completed.

10. Security

Measures in the current design include HTTPS, browser security headers, authenticated Supabase sessions, Row Level Security, bounded inputs, server-side secrets, origin checks, per-user/global AI quotas, data minimization, optional cloud processing, and account deletion. No system is perfectly secure. Users should use a unique password, protect their email account, keep devices updated, and report suspected compromise privately.

11. Children and age limits

The hosted service is intended only for adults aged 18 or older. It is not directed to children, and the operator does not knowingly collect children’s personal information. Do not create an account or enable camera processing for a child. If the operator learns that prohibited child data was collected, it will be addressed and deleted as required by law.

12. International transfers

Service providers may process data in the United States and other countries. Those countries may have different privacy protections. Required contractual and transfer safeguards must be confirmed before offering the service in a jurisdiction that requires them.

13. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

Browser “Do Not Track” signals are not consistently standardized. CogniHood does not sell data or use cross-context behavioral advertising. A recognized opt-out signal will be treated consistently with those existing practices where applicable.

14. Changes and contact

Material changes will be reflected by a new effective date and, when appropriate, an in-product notice or renewed acknowledgement. Continued use after an effective change may be subject to the updated notice where permitted by law.

Current public contact: CogniHood repository. A verified private privacy-request channel must be added before commercial launch.
