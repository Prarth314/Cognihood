Reporting claimed infringement

A copyright owner or authorized agent may report material believed to infringe copyright. A complete notice should include:

  1. a physical or electronic signature of the authorized person;
  2. identification of the copyrighted work, or a representative list for multiple works;
  3. identification and exact location of the material claimed to be infringing;
  4. contact information sufficient to reach the complaining party;
  5. a statement of good-faith belief that the disputed use is not authorized by the owner, agent, or law; and
  6. a statement that the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that the sender is authorized to act for the owner.

Contact the maintainer through the method listed on the Prarth314 GitHub profile to request a private delivery channel. Do not place addresses, signatures, phone numbers, identity documents, or other sensitive notice contents in a public issue.

Counter-notice

If material is removed because of a copyright complaint and you believe removal was mistaken or based on misidentification, request a private channel and provide the information required by applicable law, which may include your signature, identification of removed material and its prior location, a statement under penalty of perjury, consent to appropriate court jurisdiction, and contact information. Legal consequences can arise from false notices or counter-notices; consider consulting counsel.

Repeat infringement

Where appropriate and legally required, the operator may terminate accounts of repeat infringers and remove or disable access to disputed material while a claim is reviewed.

No registered DMCA agent yet. CogniHood does not currently publish a registered U.S. Copyright Office designated agent and does not claim that this page alone establishes DMCA safe-harbor eligibility. Before hosting user-uploaded public content commercially, the operator must register and publish an agent and adopt a verified notice-and-takedown workflow.
